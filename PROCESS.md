# Development Process – Between the Pages

## 1. Starting Idea

The idea for *Between the Pages* came from my interest in reading and the personal memories connected to books.

A traditional digital bookshelf is mainly used to store and organize book information. However, I wanted to explore a more personal and emotional way of interacting with books. I imagined a small virtual library where each book could represent a memory, a conversation, or a particular moment in someone's life.

In this experience, users can collect books, revisit their reading experiences, meet visitors in the library, and record their thoughts in a personal journal.

My goal was to create a relaxing and storybook-like experience rather than another productivity tool. I wanted the interaction to feel gentle, playful, and personal.

## 2. Design Decisions

I first developed the visual direction in Figma. I chose a warm pastel colour palette, hand-drawn illustrations, rounded interface elements, and soft typography to create a comfortable atmosphere.

The visual design was important to the concept because I wanted the library to feel like a personal space rather than a conventional digital application.

I decided to organize the experience around several main scenes:

- An entry screen that introduces the experience.
- A virtual library where users can explore their books.
- Book detail views that provide more information about individual books.
- Visitor interactions that allow users to have conversations.
- A daily journal where users can record their thoughts and memories.
- A closing scene that gives the experience a gentle ending.

During development, I used Figma Make to help turn the original visual designs into an interactive prototype. I focused on connecting the different screens and making the main buttons and interactions functional, while trying to preserve the original visual style.

One important decision was to prioritize the basic user journey before adding more decorative effects. I wanted users to be able to enter the library, explore books, interact with the interface, and return to their journal without getting lost.

## 3. Development Changes and Iteration

One challenge was translating static visual designs into functional interactions.

A screen that looks complete in Figma does not necessarily work as an interactive experience. For example, a bookshelf needs a clear clickable area, a book needs to open the correct detail view, and users need a way to return to the previous scene.

I therefore focused on connecting the main navigation and interaction states instead of treating every screen as an independent design.

I also considered how users could keep their personal reading information. This led me to include book collection and journal-saving interactions, with local storage as the intended approach for keeping information between visits.

Another important adjustment was deciding not to let AI freely redesign the interface. Some automatically generated layouts and visual suggestions may be convenient, but they can make the result look different from my original concept. I chose to keep the original colour palette, illustrations, and overall atmosphere as the main visual reference.

## 4. Testing and Feedback

I used the Figma Make preview to test the prototype during development.

My testing focused on the basic user journey:

1. Entering the experience from the opening screen.
2. Moving into the library.
3. Selecting and exploring books.
4. Checking whether the main navigation was understandable.
5. Opening the journal and checking the writing experience.
6. Returning to other scenes and closing the library.

During this process, I paid attention to whether the interactions were clear and whether the different screens felt connected.

One issue I identified was that visual elements alone do not always communicate their function. A button or illustrated object may look interactive, but users still need clear feedback after clicking it. This made me pay more attention to clickable areas, navigation feedback, and the consistency of interactions.

At this stage, the testing was mainly self-testing through the prototype preview. I have not yet completed a formal user study with external participants, so further testing with classmates or other readers would be useful.

## 5. Use of AI Tools

I used AI tools as part of the design and development process.

### Figma AI / Figma Make

I used Figma AI and Figma Make to help translate the visual concept into an interactive web prototype. They helped with interface implementation, connecting screens, and developing the basic interaction structure.

### ChatGPT

I used ChatGPT to organize the project requirements, plan the interaction flow, write and refine prompts for Figma Make, and troubleshoot development problems. It also helped me prepare project documentation and understand how to organize the source code for GitHub submission.

### AI Suggestions: What I Kept

One useful suggestion was to build the experience in smaller, testable stages rather than asking AI to generate the entire game at once.

I kept this approach because it made it easier to identify problems, check individual interactions, and make changes without affecting the whole experience.

### AI Suggestions: What I Changed or Rejected

AI sometimes suggested replacing visual elements or simplifying the interface with more conventional web design patterns.

I did not follow these suggestions directly. I wanted to preserve the original visual identity of the project, especially its pastel colours, hand-drawn illustrations, and storybook atmosphere.

Instead, I treated the original Figma design as the visual reference and used AI mainly to help implement the interactions.

This experience taught me that AI can speed up development, but it should not make every creative decision. I still needed to evaluate its suggestions based on the original design intention.

## 6. Reflection and Future Improvements

Through this project, I learned that designing an interactive experience involves more than creating attractive screens. The visual design, interaction logic, and feedback need to work together.

I also realized that a personal library can be more than a place to organize books. It can become a space for remembering, reflecting, and reconnecting with past reading experiences.

If I continue developing this project, I would like to improve the visitor dialogue system, make the journal experience more personalized, and introduce more subtle sound and animation details.

I would also like to conduct more user testing to understand whether the experience feels intuitive and emotionally engaging to other readers.

Overall, this project helped me better understand the process of turning a visual concept into an interactive experience, and how to use AI as a supporting tool while maintaining my own design decisions.
